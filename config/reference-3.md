Let’s talk strategy, and I will talk particularly from my own experience and field of expertise - algorithmic trading. I’ll break down some of the common ways in which algorithms are used.


At Remi Technologies we build algorithms for everyday investors, but it can be hard for clients to conceptualise their ideas. Here are some strategies:

The Crypto Tiger Shark

I have only ever had cryptocurrency traders talk to me about this one, I call it the Tiger Shark.

The Tiger Shark involves trying to replicate day trading patterns within a single cryptocurrency pair. If you’ve ever really actively day traded crypto or Forex you will be familiar with the nature of falling into a groove. You enter a Buy, then you look for an exit, and you sell. Then look for the next Buying opportunity. It’s scalping in some regards, but simplified, objectified, and reiterated continuously. The algorithm looks only for an entry, and then only for an exit. There are no concurrent positions, there are no partial exits.

This is one of the most popular types of algorithms we make because it’s lightweight (also cheap for the client), and easily modified later.
Advanced-Autonomous Equity Screening

AAES is the favourite algorithm of value investors that don’t want to commit to execution.

The AAES algorithm runs remotely and effectively scans the market for undervalued stocks. Sounds simple? Where it gets complex is in how it determines which stocks are undervalued. This algorithm is capable to be geared towards dividend yield-seeking investors, or long-term holders looking for bargains. It doesn’t just conduct ratio analysis, it also compiles the tools needed to the investor to make a decision on their own for whether it’s worth investigating a stock further or not. No, it doesn’t just spew out 100 “great stocks that match your conditions”. It’s not a screener, it’s a management tool powered by intelligence augmentation.
Sentiment Mining

It doesn’t matter if you’re right about something if the market never realizes it.

Sentiment mining allows you to gain an understanding of what both retail investors and institutional investors are saying about particular stocks and monitors when price-action is responsive to it the most. I don’t want to go into the details too much (it’s proprietary), but there are ways to tell when institutional volume drops out and retail sentiment affects price-action significantly more.
Stealth Trading

Now we’re in professional territory. Stealth trading is all about remaining low-key in the markets.

The first stealth trading algorithm we developed was for an individual with an affinity for penny stocks. They wanted a way to place their trades in ways so as not to alert the market of their presence. On the other side of stealth trading, we have traders trying to spot sneaky entrances and exits from positions.

You’ve heard of High-Frequency Trading? This is a lite version of that.

I’ll be honest. No client has ever asked us to build this. But we’ve played around with the technology used in cross-exchange arbitrage, and are waiting to implement this for the first reluctant person to ask us to. Simple Technical Scoring

Take of the indicators that you use in your strategy and score them.

I’m not a fan of using purely statistics to run a trading algorithm and think that it’s best done in conjunction with other forms of algorithmic analysis. However, there are many people that still use strategies like MACD combined with SARs to govern 99% of their trades.
These are just some strategies that I’ve seen used. However, this doesn’t even scratch the surface of algorithmic trading (even on a retail level).

Stop thinking that algorithmic trading is reserved for Wall Street, nowadays even an investor with no experience with programming can trade algorithmically. We’re happy to help anyone that’s interested in dipping their toe in the quant side of the trading pool.
